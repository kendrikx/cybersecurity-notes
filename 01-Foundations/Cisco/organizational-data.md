# Cisco Introduction to Cybersecurity
## Organizational Data & Security Models

---

# Types of Organizational Data

Organizations generate and maintain different forms of data.

---

# 1. Traditional Data

## Transactional Data
Detailed records of events or exchanges.

Examples:
- purchases
- deposits
- payments

---

## Intellectual Property

Sensitive organizational information that provides competitive advantage.

Examples:
- patents
- trademarks
- product plans
- trade secrets

Loss of intellectual property can seriously damage an organization.

---

## Financial Data

Financial records that show organizational performance.

Examples:
- income statements
- balance sheets
- cash flow statements

---

# 2. Internet of Things (IoT) & Big Data

IoT refers to physical devices connected to the internet using:
- sensors
- software
- communication technologies

These devices exchange and collect large amounts of data.

Examples:
- smart devices
- cameras
- industrial sensors
- smart home systems

---

# The McCumber Cube Security Model

Created in 1991 to help organizations establish and maintain information security.

The model contains three dimensions:
- Security Attributes (The What)
- State of Data (The Where)
- Safeguards (The How)

---

# Dimension 1: Security Attributes (The What)

## Confidentiality
Prevents unauthorized disclosure of information.

Only authorized individuals should access sensitive data.

---

## Integrity
Ensures information remains:
- accurate
- complete
- trustworthy

---

## Availability
Ensures reliable and timely access to data and systems.

Methods include:
- backups
- system maintenance
- updates

---

# Dimension 2: State of Data (The Where)

Data must be protected in all states.

---

## Data in Process
Data actively being used or modified in:
- CPU
- RAM

---

## Data at Rest
Stored data located on:
- hard drives
- flash drives
- cloud storage

---

## Data in Transit
Data moving between systems or networks.

---

# Dimension 3: Safeguards (The How)

---

## Technology (Technical Safeguards)

Security tools and systems used to protect data.

Examples:
- monitoring systems
- firewalls
- security software

---

## Policies and Practices (Administrative Safeguards)

Rules and procedures defining secure handling of data.

Examples:
- security policies
- access control procedures
- organizational guidelines

---

## Education & Awareness

Training users and employees to:
- recognize threats
- follow security policies
- avoid phishing attacks

---

# Consequences of a Security Breach

Possible impacts include:
- reputational damage
- vandalism
- theft
- revenue loss
- intellectual property damage
